title: The UI Guy I Am Not (But Tried to Be)

---

I am a backend person. Always have been. Give me a system to design, a pipeline to build, a bug to trace through three services. That is where I feel at home. But a portfolio website has been long overdue, and for some reason I decided I was going to build the UI myself.

Not copy a template. Not hand it off to my friend who is genuinely talented at this. Just me, Excalidraw, and a lot of iteration.

Here is how that went.

## It started with a job application

I was filling out a form for a role I wanted to apply for. One of the fields asked for a portfolio link. I did not have one. I submitted the form anyway, did not want to delay it, but that same night I told my friend I wanted to build one.

We went to his place, had some food. He was already ready, knew exactly why I was there. But he also had work the next morning and was heading out. He asked me, "Do you need it today or tomorrow?"

I could not bring myself to say I need it now. He would have done it without hesitating, that is just the kind of person he is, but asking that felt selfish. So I said, let me try it on my own. If it does not work, I will come back to you.

He walked me through how to go about it, and then I just went for it.

## That first night was rough

I made some genuinely bad iterations. Disappointed in myself, disappointed in the model I was using. Nothing looked like what was in my head.

Woke up the next morning. Tried again. Got fed up. Deleted the whole repo and started from scratch.

**That reset helped.**

## The Excalidraw sketch

After all the AI back-and-forth, I stopped trying to generate my way into a design. Opened Excalidraw and just started drawing. No references, no inspiration boards. Just: what would actually make sense for a page that represents me?

That sketch became the layout. Everything you see on the site came from that drawing session, not from anything I found online.

## The parts that were actually hard

The katana slider nearly broke me. I wanted a slider that makes a katana-drawing sound as you move it. References for something like that basically did not exist. Used Claude to build it, and it kept getting it wrong in slightly different ways each time. The version live now works, but the katana itself does not look like a proper katana yet. That is on the list.

The font took longer than I expected too. I had a more relaxed, casual font originally that felt right for how I am personally. Switched to sans-serif in the end, it felt more honest to how I actually work. Serious about what I build, but the katana slider is still right there to remind you I also find this stuff fun.

## What it actually is

A home page with a basic intro. A projects page. A blogs section, this is the second one, more are coming. And a Ping me page where you drop your name, email, and socials, hit send, and it lands in my inbox directly. No friction.

There is also a manga-themed image of me that overglorifies me significantly. I am keeping it.

## It is going to change

My friend looked at it when it was done and said what I already knew: there will be more iterations. He showed me his first portfolio and told me he has gone through ten to fifteen versions since. This is version one. The project cards will become clickable pages. The katana will look like an actual katana. The whole thing might look completely different in a year.

That is fine.

## Why it mattered to do it myself

I could have had a better-looking portfolio if I had let my friend build it. He is genuinely good at this and I am not.

But there is something about not being able to do something yourself that bothers me. Not in a way that means I need to be good at everything, I do not. But for something that represents me, that is going to be the first thing a recruiter or another builder sees, I wanted to know I built it.

Even the corny parts.
